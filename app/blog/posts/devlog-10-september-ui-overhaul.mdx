By September the product worked, and it looked like it had been built by six different people. In a way it had: many AI sessions over many months, each with its own idea of what a card looks like.

## Try one: 12 September

The plan started with an honest verdict: the UI was in a very bad state. Two problems were easy to see. Cards that used to have a border had lost it. And empty states were wrong: with no contracts, the "nothing here" box floated in the middle of the screen, and other cards showed edges where they shouldn't.

A font experiment came and went in minutes. Geist Mono looked silly and the normal font came back. Fast reversals are fine. That's what cheap experiments are for.

Then a long overhaul plan, drawn from real designs, MCP servers and skills, with a proper task list. And a new feature to me: **goals.** In Codex, you set a goal and the tool keeps nudging the AI to continue on its own. The logs show dozens of automatic "keep working toward the goal" notices in a row with nobody around. That's what I wanted, and also a bit scary. Two things helped: saying what to skip (UYAP and the field module), and saying when to stop.

## Try two: 15 September

I moved to another tool (Muse) and picked up the same plan, mostly done by then. The handoff worked only because the plan lived in a file, not in a chat.

A UI-skills MCP server joined the project, after a "disabled" error at first. Then the design skills gave the project a score, and it was low. Uncomfortable, and useful. The other big realization was an old one from June: every component is shared, so a fix can spread across all 88 pages at once. That's the whole reason to build shared components.

## Try three: the design lab

18 September: a separate workspace to design in, so experiments couldn't break the main app. I liked the isolation and disliked the result. The lab had drifted away from the real product: pretty, but a fictional app.

The correction was to keep the isolation and use the real components, with HeroUI and Kumo UI as inputs, plus a separate page listing every screen so I could see them without logging in.

The rule I took: **design in isolation, but with real components.** Otherwise you're comparing a mockup with a product.

## Then Claude, and tidying

On 24 September, in Claude Code, the first job was the tools themselves: some of the skills I'd installed with `npx` hadn't come across. Then:

- The migration tool only understood Microsoft SQL Server, so MySQL and PostgreSQL adapters were next.
- A dirty branch got sorted into commits before anything new started.
- The design got re-scored, and the site moved onto a handful of shared components: selects and dates first, then headings and cards.
- One idea that grew on me: turn the case-tracking page into something closer to Evernote. Notes on a case feel good to a user. The 25 September commit is called `case-file margin notes on paper`.

## The end of the week

On 25 September CI moved onto a Playwright image, since the tests had been slow and hard on my laptop. A 20-phase plan started with phase 0. And on-prem got a decision: Tailscale shouldn't be required. The `install.sh` commit at the end of the day hands the office over to a `/kurulum` (setup) page.

Then I stopped, because I was tired. The AI wasn't. That's worth remembering.

## What I took from this

- Put the plan in a file. Every tool handoff worked because of it.
- Let the AI run on a goal, but decide in advance what it skips and when it stops.
- A design lab should use the real components.
- Being tired is a valid reason to stop.

That's the story up to now. Kolay Büro keeps growing, and I'll keep writing.
